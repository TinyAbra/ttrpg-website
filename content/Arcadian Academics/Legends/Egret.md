Egret is a tragic play that is most commonly known by how the floorboards of the stage 'scream' in protest from soaking in fake blood. It is the story of a young woman who refuses to follow her fate by any means necessary and pays the ultimate price. 

### The Facts:
The story of the commoner damsel is popular across the Sorvian Coast, and can be recognized by three distinct features: the kindly godparent, a chance meeting with nobility, and a jealous rival. Egret, despite having these three features, does not follow the typical structure of the commoner damsel story after the appearance of The Trickster - a figure which can be implied to be Kalyani, Oubos, or a djinn. 

#### The Fable:
Egret: A Very Morbid Tragedy

Act 1: Egret's merchant father remarries, the second wife has two daughters. Egret and her Sisters get along fine, at first, but as if possessed, start to torment her after the announcement of a royal ball. The Stepmother wants to prevent her from attending, so Egret attends with her Godfather and Cousin. The Prince "falls in love at first sight" which... his fiancé wants nothing to do with, and neither does Egret. The festivities last seven days, and after 6 days of refusing the prince, a malicious Spirit takes over her body and agrees to the marriage.

No one is Happy. 

Act 2: Egret enlists her Cousin and her Sisters to help her escape The Prince. Prince finds out her Cousin is trying to get her out of the city and has him executed for treason. Scared of the consequences, the Sisters involve their mother, who disowns them for their actions and turns them in. The Sisters commit suicide in prison. Egret attempts to hang herself, but is stopped by the Spirit. 

The Wedding is Lovely.

Act 3: Egret murders her Stepmother for having betrayed her Sisters. Her father, who witnesses this, dies of shock. An unstable Egret, fighting against a Spirit that insists getting married is best for her, goes through with the wedding. She poisons the attendants except for her husband. The Prince's eyes are gouged out "to remove his love for her" and left to die in the room of corpses. Egret flees, still pursued by the Spirit. Slowly, Egret goes insane as she walks to the border, and kills herself five steps from freedom.
